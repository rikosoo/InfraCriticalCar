\section{How each parameter was set}

The model carries four kinds of parameter. This section states how each kind was
arrived at, so that a reader can judge every value and a panel can replace it.

\paragraph{Initial-access probabilities $p(e)$ for steps out of the actor.}
Ordered by how often the corresponding entry vector appears in the corpus and in
public reporting on manufacturing intrusions. Phishing a supplier is placed
above phishing an OEM because suppliers in the corpus are compromised with
weaker controls; a trojanised vendor software package is placed near the bottom
because no corpus incident used one. The ordering is the claim; the absolute
values are the coarse bands of \autoref{tab:s2}.

\paragraph{Lateral and descent probabilities.} Set from the technical
precondition of the step. A step that needs only credentials the actor already
holds sits in the H or VH band (a domain member authenticating to its own
directory, an engineering workstation downloading a program, a controller
driving its own process). A step that needs an exploit sits in M. A step that
needs physical presence, a safety key switch or a signed project sits in L.

\paragraph{Detection probabilities $\delta(e)$.} These encode the monitoring a
mid-sized OEM plant can be assumed to have \emph{before} any control from the
catalogue is deployed, which is why $S_0$ is a baseline and not an unprotected
configuration. Steps inside the corporate estate, where endpoint logging is
normal, carry higher values than steps inside the cell, where protocol-aware
monitoring is the exception. $\delta$ is read as detected \emph{and} contained
within the actor's dwell time; an alert nobody acts on is not containment.

\paragraph{Consequence parameters.} Anchored on reported outcomes rather than on
judgement: the one-day, 14-plant Toyota stoppage at one end of the duration
distribution and the five-week Jaguar Land Rover outage at the other. The loss
rate per stopped hour and the fixed response costs are the coarsest parameters
in the model and the ones a plant should replace first with its own figures.

\paragraph{What the values are not.} They are not measurements, and no claim is
made that they are the parameters of any specific plant. Experiment~E6 of the
paper reports how far the conclusions move when every probability is perturbed;
conclusions that do not survive that perturbation are not drawn.
\texttt{docs/elicitation-protocol.md} in the artefact gives the instrument and
procedure for replacing these estimates with a panel's.

\section{Reproducing the results}

The artefact at \url{\capmrepo} regenerates every number, table and figure of
the paper. It needs only a CPython 3.9 or newer interpreter and no third-party
package.

\begin{verbatim}
git clone <repository>
cd InfraCriticalCar
python3 experiments/run_all.py            # E1-E7, about ten minutes
python3 experiments/build_paper_assets.py # tables and figures, both languages
python3 experiments/build_supplementary.py
python3 -m pytest                         # 65 tests
\end{verbatim}

Monte Carlo experiments use 20{,}000 campaigns per configuration and a fixed
seed (20260916); simulation is deterministic across processes and platforms,
which a test in the suite enforces. \texttt{experiments/results/summary.json}
carries every number the paper quotes, and \texttt{data/incidents.csv} carries
the corpus with the source URL and consultation date of each record.
